[R2-05] "the amplification polynomial": why is it called like this?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Location: Chapter 2, Section 2.4.4 | Printed Page: P38 | TeX Line: 1857
- Context:
  "we obtain the amplification polynomial
  (1+\alpha+2\beta\delta)\xi^2 - 2(1-\sigma^2\delta)\xi + (1-\alpha-2\beta\delta) = 0,"
- Why flagged:
  Reviewer 2 asks: "why is it called like this?". The text introduces \xi as the
  amplification factor (u_j^{n+1} = \xi u_j^n), but does not state that setting the
  characteristic equation to zero defines the characteristic polynomial whose roots
  are precisely the amplification factors.
- Suggested Change (in RED, no em-dashes):
  "we obtain the characteristic equation for the amplification factor $\xi$
  (commonly termed the \emph{amplification polynomial} because its algebraic roots
  $\xi$ define the modal growth or decay factor per time step, $u_j^{n+1} = \xi u_j^n$
  \cite{...}):
  \begin{equation*}
  (1+\alpha+2\beta\delta)\xi^2 - 2(1-\sigma^2\delta)\xi + (1-\alpha-2\beta\delta) = 0,
  \end{equation*}"
- Reference to Search:
  * J. C. Strikwerda, "Finite Difference Schemes and Partial Differential Equations",
    Wadsworth & Brooks/Cole, 2004.
  * C. Hirsch, "Numerical Computation of Internal and External Flows", Wiley, 2007.
- Existing References in Thesis_bibliography.bib you could use:
  * bilbao_numerical_2009 (Chapter 2 & 4 covers von Neumann analysis, amplification
    factors, and the characteristic amplification polynomial for difference schemes).

[R2-06] "explicit leapfrog FDTD" and "first-order Velocity Verlet scheme"
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Location: Chapter 3, Section 3.2.3 | Printed Page: P87 | TeX Line: 4413
- Context:
  "For the purely undamped wave equation, standard explicit leapfrog FDTD is
  mathematically equivalent to a first-order Velocity Verlet scheme, making the
  Strang-split ARD integration straightforward."
- Why flagged:
  Reviewer 2 notes: "'explicit leapfrog FDTD' and 'first-order Velocity Verlet scheme'
  are not defined".
- Suggested Change (in RED, no em-dashes):
  "For the purely undamped wave equation, standard explicit leapfrog FDTD (which
  staggers the evaluation of displacements at integer time steps $t^n$ and velocities
  at half-integer steps $t^{n+1/2}$) is mathematically equivalent to the symplectic
  Velocity Verlet scheme recast in first-order state-space form \cite{...}
  (a Kick-Drift-Kick integrator updating states via
  $v^{n+1/2} = v^n + \frac{\Delta t}{2} a(u^n)$, $u^{n+1} = u^n + \Delta t v^{n+1/2}$,
  $v^{n+1} = v^{n+1/2} + \frac{\Delta t}{2} a(u^{n+1})$), making the Strang-split
  ARD integration straightforward."
- Reference to Search:
  * W. C. Swope, H. C. Andersen, P. H. Berens, K. R. Wilson, "A computer simulation
    method for the calculation of equilibrium constants", J. Chem. Phys. 76(1), 1982
    (original Velocity Verlet formulation).
  * E. Hairer, C. Lubich, G. Wanner, "Geometric Numerical Integration: Structure-Preserving
    Algorithms for Ordinary Differential Equations", Springer, 2006 (Section II.1 on
    symplectic splitting and leapfrog/Verlet equivalence).
- Existing References in Thesis_bibliography.bib you could use:
  * bilbao_numerical_2009 (defines standard leapfrog FDTD in acoustics).
  * Velocity Verlet is not currently in the bib file and requires adding a citation
    such as Swope (1982) or Hairer (2006).
