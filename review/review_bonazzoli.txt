Report on the PhD thesis “Domain
Decomposition and Differentiable Physics for
the Viscoelastic Wave Equation”
by Gerardo Cicalese
Reviewer: Marcella Bonazzoli
Overall comment.
I have found the contents of the PhD thesis "Domain
Decomposition and Differentiable Physics for the Viscoelastic Wave Equation"
by Gerardo Cicalese very interesting, and reading the manuscript has been a
rewarding experience: the various topics covered by the thesis are clearly ex-
plained, the needed background is correctly recalled, and the novelties contained
in each chapter are easy to detect. The thesis is a balanced interplay between
theory and simulations: all the theoretical results are validated by extensive
numerical experiments. The thesis deals with cutting edge problems for the nu-
merical simulation of applications related to computational acoustics, incorpo-
rating also atmospheric absorption. The studied model is the (time-dependent)
damped wave equation with both telegrapher and viscoelastic damping terms.
The original contributions contained in the thesis range from theoretical results
(in particular, the stability/numerical dispersion analysis of the FDTD and
PSTD methods in Chapter 2, the convergence analysis of the OSWR method
and the extension of the ARD method to the damped case in Chapter 3) to
the implementation of the developed tools, as well as their application to sound
field reconstruction by incorporating them into a Differentiable Physics Neural
Network architecture in Section 3.3. It is remarkable that the candidate was
able to successfully and significantly contribute to the knowledge advance in
three research domains that are often studied separately: stable discretization
methods, efficient space-time solvers, and neural networks for inverse problems.
As a general assessment, I consider the thesis as a very significant and excel-
lent contribution to the knowledge advance in a field where several outstanding
researchers are working actively. In view of the above considerations, I believe
that the PhD thesis by Gerardo Cicalese conforms to the standards of a high
quality international thesis, and it is my pleasure to recommend that he be
admitted to the final defense of his doctoral dissertation.
Detailed comments.
The PhD manuscript contains 4 chapters, where the
last one summarizes the thesis contributions and mentions future research lines.
In order to highlight the significance of Gerardo Cicalese’s research, I will com-
ment on each chapter separately.
Chapter 1 is a comprehensive introduction and literature review about both
the computational acoustics background and the numerical methods studied
in the thesis. The drawbacks of classical geometrical techniques highlight the

need for methods that simulate the wave propagation model. Applications such
as room acoustics simulation require to include time-dependent behavior and
atmospheric absorption in the model, and to accelerate wave-based methods,
which is the objective of the thesis.
Chapter 2 is devoted to the physical model, the definition of benchmark prob-
lems and the analysis of two discretization methods: Finite-Difference Time-
Domain (FDTD) and Pseudo-Spectral Time-Domain (PSTD). I have especially
appreciated the discussion of several physics-related aspects: the choice of tele-
grapher and viscoelastic damping terms, the derivation of the dispersion rela-
tion, and the study of the absorbing properties of Robin boundary conditions.
The modal expansion of the solution gives indications about the instability of
the inverse problem of Chapter 3, when damping is present. This is a crucial
and mathematically challenging aspect. Moreover, the careful definition of 6
benchmark problems for the 1D undamped and damped equations will be very
useful for the community to test the performance of new methods. The most
substantial original contributions of Chapter 2 consist in extending FDTD and
PSTD to the damped case and rigorously analyzing them.
For FDTD, the
candidate describes in detail the algorithmic treatment of all boundary con-
ditions, and proves that the scheme is consistent and stable under the CFL
condition. PSTD, which applies to the case of homogeneous Neumann bound-
ary conditions, is based on expanding the solution in a spectral basis, so that the
resulting uncoupled modal equations can be solved using an exact time integra-
tor. The time integrator in the general damped case is far from being a trivial
extension of the undamped one. The candidate proves unconditional stability
and conducts error and dispersion analyses. Extensive numerical experiments
on the 6 benchmarks validate the convergence theory.
Chapter 3 explores two space-time domain decomposition techniques (Op-
timized Schwarz Waveform Relaxation, OSWR, and Adaptive Rectangular De-
composition, ARD) for the forward model, and the application of the developed
tools to sound field reconstruction. The main focus for OSWR is the compu-
tation of its convergence factor, for 1D/2D overlapping two-domain configura-
tions, in order to optimize the parameters of the Robin transmission conditions
at the subdomain interfaces.
Numerical experiments study how the optimal
transmission parameters and convergence factor depend on the damping coef-
ficients. As the viscoelastic damping increases, choosing optimal parameters
becomes more important to accelerate the convergence. While PSTD cannot
be used in OSWR, both FDTD and PSTD can be used in ARD. Indeed, in
ARD the non-overlapping subdomains evolve independently under homogeneous
Neumann conditions, and subdomains coupling is imposed by a corrective force
acting on their interfaces. Stability and convergence properties are illustrated
numerically in 1D, and even a 3D simulation is performed. Finally, the candi-
date combines the new techniques with a neural network architecture to solve a
challenging unstable inverse problem. High-frequency artifacts are suppressed
thanks to a carefully chosen regularization term.
The initial condition and
the viscoelastic coefficient are successfully reconstructed, while the telegrapher
damping coefficient is not. Nevertheless, these numerical results are impressive
and show the applicative potential of the developed mathematical and numerical
technologies.
